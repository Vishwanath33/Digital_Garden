## The page and the background

The Lorenz attractor runs behind every page, and the text sits on a sheet of
paper laid over it. Matthew Butterick's rule in *Practical Typography* is to
divide a page into foreground and background and never let the background
upstage the foreground; the sheet is how that rule is kept here. It is
opaque enough that the text reads as if on plain paper, and translucent
enough that the lines show faintly through it, while the attractor's two
lobes open out on either side, where nothing needs reading.

## The text

Butterick's first maxim is to decide the body text before anything else,
because there is more of it than of anything else, and four choices settle
it: size, line length, line spacing and font.

- **Size.** 20 pixels, in the middle of his 15–25 pixel range for the web.
- **Measure.** About 66 characters, Robert Bringhurst's ideal from *The
  Elements of Typographic Style* (anything from 45 to 75 is comfortable).
- **Leading.** 145%, the top of Butterick's 120–145% range, because the
  typeface is small on its body.
- **Face.** [Newsreader](https://github.com/productiontype/Newsreader), a
  text serif drawn for reading on screens, with optical sizes that tighten
  its display cuts for titles. Labels, numbers and captions are set in
  [IBM Plex Mono](https://github.com/IBM/plex). The two never mix: serif
  for the text, monospace for the apparatus around it.

Paragraphs are indented rather than spaced, as in a book, and the first
after a heading is set flush. Text is set ragged right with hyphenation
rather than justified, because justification on screen opens rivers the
browser cannot close.

## Margins and notes

The sheet keeps a wide margin to the right of the text, and footnotes become
sidenotes there, beside the line that cites them, as in Edward Tufte's
books. When the sheet is too narrow for a margin, they fold out inline when
their number is tapped.

## The background

A live integration of the Lorenz system at σ = 10, ρ = 28, β = 8/3, stepped
with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, so the figure is the real attractor rather
than a drawing of one. A faint atlas, a single long trajectory, shows its
whole shape; three live trajectories that begin 10⁻⁵ apart trace over it and
drift into three paths within seconds. In the light theme it is drawn in
ink, in the dark theme in light.

It keeps out of the way. *Still*, in the header, stops it and remembers the
choice; under `prefers-reduced-motion` it never moves at all; it rests while
the tab is hidden; and if it ever starts costing frames it gives up
resolution, then the length of its trails, then its motion.
[The entry on the attractor](/notes/lorenz-attractor/) says why it is there.

## The apparatus

- **Accession numbers** are given in the order entries were written, as a
  library numbers what it acquires; the call number adds the shelf.
- **Catalogue cards** close every entry, with the entry's subjects as
  numbered tracings, as on the cards in a library's drawers, and with what
  it cites and what cites it. The citations are computed when the site is
  built, by reading every entry's links.
- **The map** is a force-directed graph drawn to a canvas: repulsion between
  every pair of nodes, springs along links, a weak pull to the centre. It
  settles and then stops, so an idle map costs nothing.
- **Search** (press <kbd>/</kbd>) runs in your browser over a small index
  built with the site. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

## Build

Static HTML generated by [Eleventy](https://www.11ty.dev/) from Markdown
files. A push to the repository builds it with a GitHub Actions workflow,
and GitHub Pages serves it. No framework, no analytics, no cookies, no
third-party requests; the fonts are served from this site. The only thing
stored in your browser is your theme choice, and only if you change it.

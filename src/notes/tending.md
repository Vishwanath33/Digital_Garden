Most writing about digital gardens is about why to have one. Almost none is
about the maintenance, which is the part that decides whether it survives
past three months. These are working rules, held loosely, mostly derived from
having broken them.

## Revise on contact, not on schedule

I do not set aside time to tend. Tending happens when a note comes up in
something else I am reading or arguing about — that is the moment I actually
have the new information, and the moment the friction of editing is lowest.
Scheduled gardening sessions produced cosmetic edits and nothing else.

## Split when a section starts arguing

A note should have one claim. When a section inside it grows its own thesis,
its own objections, and its own sources, it has become a note and should be
pulled out and linked to. The tell is usually that I have written the phrase
"but of course" in it.

## Let notes die visibly

Some notes turn out to be wrong, or to be about nothing. The temptation is to
delete them. Better: demote the stage, replace the body with a short note
saying what was wrong and what replaced it, and keep the URL alive so
incoming links still land. A dead note that explains its own death is more
useful than a 404.[^linkrot]

[^linkrot]: This also means never reusing a slug for a different subject,
which is the one form of link rot entirely within my control.

## The backlink is the unit of value

When I add a note, the useful question is not "is this good" but "what does
this connect to". A note with no backlinks after a month is usually a note I
wrote for the pleasure of having written it. Sometimes that is fine. Usually
it means the idea belonged inside an existing note as a paragraph.

## Open questions

Genuinely unresolved, which is why this note is a seedling:

- Should the feed publish on every tending, or only on substantial ones? Every
  tending is honest and noisy. I currently publish everything and suspect
  that is wrong.
- Is there a point at which the number of notes makes the link graph less
  navigable rather than more? [[digital-gardens|A garden, not a blog]] assumes linking scales. I
  have no evidence that it does past a few hundred notes.
- How much should a note be allowed to assume the reader has read the notes
  it links to? Writing each one standalone causes enormous repetition.
